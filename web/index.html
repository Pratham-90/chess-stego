Chess Stego

Hide a secret inside a game of chess.

Your message is locked with a password, then written out as chess moves. Send the games to a friend. With the password, they turn back into your message.

    How it works
    
  
      
    
    1. Lock. Your message is encrypted with AES-256, a standard and heavily studied cipher.
      The key is made from your password by a deliberately slow recipe (scrypt), so guessing
      passwords is painfully slow.
      2. 
    
    2. One big number. The locked bytes are read as a single enormous number.
      3. 
    
    3. Moves. In every chess position the legal moves are sorted and numbered.
      If there are 20 of them, the number is divided by 20 and the remainder picks the move.
      Then the next position takes the next bite. Point at the numbered moves under the board to
      see this happen.
      4. 
    
    4. Back again. The receiver replays the games, reads off which numbered
      move was played each time, rebuilds the big number and unlocks it with the password.
      5. 
  
    
  
    What to know before you trust it
    
  
      
    
    • In the random style the moves come straight from scrambled data, so the games are
      full of blunders. In our tests a simple program told them apart from real games more
      than 99% of the time.
      • 
    
    • The natural style only plays moves a chess engine rates highly, and prefers the better
      ones about as often as people do. That is far more convincing, but the desktop version
      of this style was still spotted about three times in four. Treat the chess as a
      disguise against a casual glance, not against an expert.
      • 
    
    • Nobody can read the message without the password, disguise or no disguise. Use a long
      one: anyone who guesses it can read everything.
      • 
    
    • Change even one move and the message refuses to open, rather than opening wrongly.
